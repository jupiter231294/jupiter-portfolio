(03) The starting point

Two tools, no shared picture

Chubb already had automation dashboards. They answered narrow questions well, but nobody could see business, automation and customer KPIs together.

Real time

A monitor per use case

One screen per automation, refreshing every 10 seconds, with email alerts when a threshold was crossed.

History

Trends in another tool

Monthly automation rate, accuracy and volumes against target, in a separate analytics app.

The gap

Is today normal?

Answering it meant switching tools and reading emailed reports, with turnaround time defined differently by each team.

(04) Who it was for

One screen, two questions

Two personas, based on the roles Chubb’s existing tools were built for.

Daily · claims operations analyst

“Where did today go wrong?”

Watches claim volumes, drop-offs and SLA every day. Needs to spot an exception and find its cause in a few clicks.

Monthly · transformation lead

“Is automation paying off?”

Tracks automation coverage and exceptions month over month, and takes filtered views to leadership.

(05) Global framework · May 2021

Three layers, read top down

Every KPI sits in one layer, so a leader reads strategy first and an analyst can jump straight to process. Tiles turn red below target and black when ahead.

  • Strategy & experience: financials, automation value, NPS.
  • Customer journey: acquisition, servicing, retention, claims.
  • Capability: digital servicing, platform, automation, contact centre.

From a red tile to its cause, in four steps

Each person orders their own view

The analyst moves process to the top. The transformation lead keeps strategy first, hides KPIs they don’t track, and adds last month and year to date beside every value.

One framework, every market

The same layers and tiles carried six variants: IPL, A&H, Japan, EMEA, Digital and Digital claims. A new market meant new content, not a new layout.

(07) Japan redesign · July 2021

From brief to design in two weeks

Japan needed something simpler: one country, one business area, claims first notice of loss. The 30 June brief set four filters and four measurement areas; the card design followed on 14 July.

Filters

Region, country, business area, measurement area.

Areas

Process, volumetrics, automation, experience.

Each KPI

Value against a target, or its 12-month or 7-day average.

Every number can be traced

A time range for each question

  • Hourly, in Japan time: supervisors watching today.
  • Daily, last 7 days: the analyst’s default.
  • Monthly, last 3 months: leadership reviews.

(08) Why two layouts

A map for leaders, a gauge for the floor

The global grid and the Japan cards look different because they answer different questions for different people.

The two converged later: the Japan card became the standard and the technical dashboard reused it, while the grid stayed for the strategic view.

(09) Technical dashboard · Aug – Dec 2021

Three iterations for engineers

After comparing five layouts, I moved the technical dashboard from a heatmap to the business dashboard’s cards, so operations and engineering read KPIs the same way.

The same drill-down, for system health

17 categories, from availability to capacity, for claims FNOL and payments. Each card opens a live chart against its threshold, plus the raw log.

(10) What I’d take forward

Three lessons

01

Start from the question

Japan’s brief named the question and its defaults before any layout, and the design got simpler for it.

02

Keep one grammar

The same card and drawer served operations and engineers, so nobody relearned the screen.

03

Make numbers traceable

Definitions and raw records beside each KPI ended the debate over whose figure was right.
